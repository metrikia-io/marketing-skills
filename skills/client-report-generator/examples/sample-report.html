What we did, and why

The month in one line

Meta absorbed a 37% budget increase without losing efficiency, and that is the whole story of June. Spend went from 21,890 $ to 58,431 $ while ROAS held at 3.05x against 3.01x last month. Scaling normally costs efficiency. This time it did not, which is what makes the next increase evidence rather than hope.

Google is steady and small, and that is a choice to revisit. 23,405 $ at 3.36x, the best return in the account, and barely moved from last month. It is the cheapest acquisition here at 37 $ per purchase against Meta's 44 $, and it is running at a quarter of Meta's budget.

Where the budget goes next

  • Push Meta to 70,000 $ in July. It took a 37% increase at flat efficiency, so the next step is measured rather than a gamble.
  • Raise Google by 30%, to roughly 30,000 $. It returns more per euro than Meta and has not been tested at volume. If ROAS holds above 3x, keep going.
  • Roll both back at 2.8x blended. Below that the mix stops paying for itself, and the decision is made in advance rather than argued after.

What these numbers rest on

Every revenue figure above is what the ad platforms claim they produced, and each platform counts the sales it believes it caused. Those counts overlap, so channel revenue adds up to more than the store actually took. Blended ROAS is the one figure no attribution model can inflate: trust it first.

Crediting each channel against the cash actually collected takes reconciling at the order level, which is what Metrikia does.
