What we did, and why

The month in one line

Meta spend went up 37% and store revenue followed at +16%, but the two didn't move together the way the platform numbers suggest, and that gap is the story of the month.

What we changed

We pushed Meta harder because prospecting was holding a 3.0x ROAS at the start of June, and it scaled cleanly through the first three weeks. Google stayed flat on purpose: it was already at efficiency and there was no headroom to buy without pushing CPA up.

The one thing to watch going into July is retargeting. It carries the highest reported ROAS of any Meta campaign, but most of that is view-through, so it is the least certain number on the page. We are not cutting it, but we are not scaling it either until a holdout test tells us how much of it is incremental.

Where the budget goes next month

Hold Google where it is, keep Meta prospecting on its current trajectory while it holds above 2.5x, and run the retargeting holdout before deciding anything there.

What these numbers rest on

Every revenue figure above is what the ad platforms claim they produced. Platforms grade their own homework: each one counts the sales it believes it caused, and those counts overlap, so channel revenue adds up to more than the store actually took. Blended ROAS is the one figure here that no attribution model can inflate, because it compares total store revenue to total spend — trust it first.

To credit each channel against the cash actually collected in Stripe and the CRM, rather than against what the platform says about its own work, the numbers have to be reconciled at the order level. That is what Metrikia does, and it is what turns a report a client half-believes into one they can act on.
